# EU transactional email providers for Better Auth OTP and operational mail

**Research date:** 2026-08-13  
**Scope:** German-first email OTP, account-security notices, Quote Request confirmations, and operational messages for the first production release.  
**Evidence rule:** Primary vendor documentation and contracts only. Prices are a dated operating estimate, not a procurement quote.

## Decision

Use **Amazon SES in `eu-central-1` (Frankfurt)** as the default, provided the production gates below pass. Keep **Mailjet** as the managed fallback, but activate it only after its order/DPA confirms that Mailjet message content, recipient addresses, delivery metadata, suppression data, backups, and routine operational access remain in EU member states.

This is a deliberately conservative result. AWS gives the application the strongest public and contractual control over the region in which customer content is stored: AWS says the customer selects the Region, it will not move or replicate content outside it without agreement, and SES exposes both API and SMTP endpoints in Frankfurt. Its DPA is incorporated into the Service Terms, includes the EU SCCs when a transfer occurs, and the AWS subprocessor register identifies the processing location by selected Region. ([AWS data privacy FAQ](https://aws.amazon.com/compliance/data-privacy-faq/), [SES endpoints](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/general/latest/gr/ses.html), [AWS Service Terms](https://aws.amazon.com/service-terms/), [AWS subprocessors](https://aws.amazon.com/compliance/sub-processors/))

The tradeoff is operational work: SES needs an AWS account and IAM policy, production-access approval, regional domain verification, bounce/complaint plumbing, alarms, and an application-owned template/retry layer. That is acceptable here because the application already owns OTP security and outbox/reconciliation responsibilities, and the email adapter can remain narrow.

Mailjet is the best fallback for a small team because it combines REST API, SMTP, EU-hosted infrastructure, templates, webhooks, automatic bounce blocking, and a low-cost self-service plan. Mailjet explicitly says personal data is stored exclusively in Frankfurt and Saint-Ghislain and that its platform data is stored and processed in the EU. However, the governing Sinch DPA also allows group transfers, includes SCC/DPF mechanisms, and describes Sinch as an independent controller for necessary communications-provider functions. The product-level EU claim therefore needs to be made contractually specific before it can satisfy this project's strict gate. ([Mailjet storage locations](https://documentation.mailjet.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042712274-Where-is-my-personal-data-stored), [Mailjet infrastructure](https://www.mailjet.com/products/email-api/infrastructure/), [Sinch DPA](https://sinch.com/legal/terms-and-conditions/other-sinch-terms-conditions/data-processing-agreement/))

The gate applies to provider-side storage and processing. Delivery to a recipient's mail exchanger necessarily follows the recipient's chosen email provider and may leave the EU. That destination transfer cannot be eliminated by choosing an EU email API; the product must instead minimize OTP content and disclose email delivery appropriately.

## Gate interpretation

An EU company, an Article 28 DPA, GDPR marketing language, or SCCs are **not** proof of EU data location. A candidate passes only where public or contractual evidence covers both storage and processing of message content and personal/message metadata in a named EU member state. Limited, documented exceptions for user-requested support or legally compelled disclosure may be accepted only through the production legal review; routine support, anti-abuse, telemetry, or group access from outside the EU does not silently inherit the hosting pass.

## Recommended production shape

Use a provider-neutral server-only `TransactionalEmail` interface. Better Auth's `sendVerificationOTP` should call an application use case that accepts a semantic message—not provider types—such as `EmailOtpRequested { challengeId, normalizedRecipient, locale, expiresAt }`. The use case writes/claims a durable send intent, renders a versioned locale template, and invokes an SES adapter. Provider message IDs and delivery events are facts attached to that intent; they never become domain identifiers.

For the first release:

- Keep `de`, `en`, and `es` subject, HTML, and plain-text bodies in Git with an immutable template version. German is the fallback only when the user has no stored/supported locale; do not infer locale from the email address.
- OTP mail contains only the code, expiry, brand, reason, ignore-if-not-requested guidance, and support/security contact. It contains no Project, quote, configuration, product-interest, or behavioural data.
- Disable open and click tracking for authentication and account-security mail. OTP email has no marketing link or unsubscribe mechanism because it is a requested security message; marketing consent and marketing suppression remain separate.
- Use one transactional sending subdomain and From identity, for example `auth.<approved-domain>`, separate from future marketing. Use a separate IAM principal/API credential and configuration set/message stream for auth.
- The application owns an outbox/inbox pair. A unique constraint on semantic send intent prevents duplicate OTP sends; transient retry uses bounded exponential backoff with jitter. If the provider response is ambiguous, reconcile before resending. Provider idempotency, where available, is defense in depth rather than the source of truth.
- Persist only the minimum event fields required for security and operations. Hash or otherwise minimize recipient identifiers in routine metrics; keep the actionable suppression address in the restricted operational store. Retention follows the separately approved privacy schedule.
- Mirror hard bounces, complaints, and permanent suppressions locally so that exit does not reactivate a bad address. A provider's suppression list is an enforcement copy, not the sole record.

## Exact production gates

All gates are blocking.

### Legal and location

1. Execute/accept the current AWS Customer Agreement, Service Terms, and DPA for the production legal entity. Archive the dated documents and the SES-relevant subprocessor list.
2. Record `eu-central-1` as the only allowed SES Region. Deny SES use elsewhere with IAM/SCP controls where the account structure permits it.
3. Legal/privacy review confirms the distinction between Frankfurt customer-content processing, AWS account/control-plane data, support access, fraud/abuse processing, recipient-MX delivery, and legally compelled access. Any non-EU customer-content flow blocks launch unless explicitly approved after a transfer assessment.
4. Do not enable Pinpoint, global engagement analytics, cross-region event destinations, non-EU log export, or message-content support attachments. SES events, queues, logs, keys, and alarms stay in approved EU Regions.
5. Add the provider and its relevant subprocessors to the privacy record. Subscribe to subprocessor-change notifications and assign an owner to reassess material changes.
6. For Mailjet fallback, obtain written product-specific confirmation covering message bodies, recipient/sender addresses, headers, delivery metadata, suppressions, logs, backups, abuse/deliverability processing, and routine personnel access in named EU member states. The confirmation must reconcile the Sinch DPA's group-transfer and independent-controller clauses. Without it, Mailjet remains disabled.

### Domain, secrets, and access

7. Use a dedicated transactional subdomain. Verify it in Frankfurt; configure 2048-bit Easy DKIM where supported, a custom MAIL FROM domain, SPF without creating a second conflicting SPF record, and DMARC alignment. Start DMARC at monitored `p=none`, review aggregate reports, then move to `quarantine`/`reject` after all legitimate senders align.
8. Complete SES production-access review and validate realistic OTP and security-notice samples, expected volume, complaint handling, and bounce process. Shared IP is the default at low volume; do not buy a dedicated IP until sustained volume justifies warm-up and ownership.
9. Create a least-privilege runtime IAM role/key restricted to `ses:SendEmail`/`ses:SendRawEmail` for the approved identity, Region, and configuration set. No AWS root/static broad key in Vercel or `.env.local`; prefer workload identity where the deployment platform supports it. Rotate and audit access.

### Delivery correctness and security

10. The app persists an outbox send intent before provider invocation. Define uniqueness, retry classification, ambiguous-response handling, maximum attempts, dead-letter state, and a runbook. OTP generation, hashing, expiry, resend limits, attempt limits, and enumeration-safe responses remain Better Auth/application responsibilities.
11. Configure SES account-level suppression for hard bounces and complaints. Publish bounce, complaint, delivery, reject, rendering-failure, and delivery-delay events through an EU-region destination to an authenticated, idempotent inbox. Alert on bounce/complaint thresholds, rejection spikes, send failures, delayed OTPs, quota exhaustion, and outbox age.
12. Verify event authenticity at the AWS boundary and deduplicate every event. Store provider message ID, internal send-intent ID, event type/time, and sanitized reason. Never log OTP values or complete message bodies.
13. Disable open/click tracking for OTP and account-security messages. Prove that templates contain no tracking pixels, link rewriting, marketing content, or sensitive domain data.
14. Apply per-IP, per-account, per-normalized-address, and global resend limits before enqueue. A successful/failed request returns an enumeration-safe response. Monitor abuse without making the mail provider the rate-limit authority.

### Templates, localization, QA, and recovery

15. Code-review and snapshot-test all `de`, `en`, and `es` subjects plus HTML/plain-text bodies. Test Unicode, escaping, malicious display names, long addresses, dark/light clients, mobile widths, plaintext, and expiry wording. A missing locale/template fails closed to reviewed German copy; no provider-side unpublished edit can change production OTP content.
16. Run a pre-production deliverability matrix to Gmail, Google Workspace, Outlook/Hotmail, Apple/iCloud, GMX, WEB.DE, and a German business-domain mailbox. Confirm SPF, DKIM, DMARC alignment, From/Reply-To, code readability, latency percentiles, spam placement, bounce ingestion, and suppression behavior.
17. Test provider outage, timeout after acceptance, duplicate event, out-of-order event, hard bounce, complaint, quota exhaustion, revoked credential, and failed template rendering. Demonstrate that no blind retry sends a second OTP and that support can diagnose delivery without reading the OTP.
18. Document and rehearse the exit: export SES suppression entries and necessary delivery evidence; retain application-owned templates and send/event records; verify the same subdomain at Mailjet only during a controlled migration; switch the adapter flag; preserve suppressions; revoke SES credentials after drain/reconciliation. Sessions and Customer Account IDs remain unaffected.

## Why the other attractive choices are not defaults

- **Mailjet** minimizes engineering work, but the public product residency statement and the broader Sinch contractual transfer language are not identical. It becomes the best operational choice only when procurement closes that gap in writing.
- **Brevo** is affordable, German-friendly, and feature-complete, but its own current terms explicitly contemplate extra-EEA processing and identify non-EU maintenance entities. A French/German company presence and EU data centers do not satisfy the stricter processing gate.
- **MailerSend** has Belgian storage and a polished developer surface, yet its DPA expressly authorizes worldwide processing. The Irish representative changes supervision/contact, not the location of the US processor.
- **Postmark** has outstanding delivery operations but candidly stores data in the US.
- **Resend** has the best simple idempotency/React integration, but its Ireland region is a routing region while account and email records remain in the US.

## Recheck triggers

Re-run this decision before contracting, at least annually, and whenever a provider changes ownership, DPA, subprocessor list, data-residency feature, region, retention defaults, pricing plan, or email-event architecture. A future EU-only managed Mailjet order, AWS European Sovereign Cloud support for SES, or another provider's contractually selectable EU data plane could change the recommendation.

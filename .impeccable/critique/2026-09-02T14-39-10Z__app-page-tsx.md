## Priority issues

### [P0] The commercial endpoint is a deceptive, nonfunctional affordance

The homepage cart and “Konfiguration anfragen” lead to a route labelled “Fake Checkout”; the primary button has no submit behavior. A direct visit also produces a plausible default configuration and price. Replace the cart metaphor with a planning/quote destination; require an authenticated Project plus immutable revision; implement labelled contact fields, consent, validation, idempotency, progress, success/history, and actionable errors. Until that vertical slice exists, remove or gate the submit-looking endpoint.

### [P1] The configurator breaks the premium visual promise

Marketing imagery sells architectural depth and material realism, then the default canvas reads as a flat CAD prototype. Default to the best art-directed environment, move to a production GLB with named material slots and explicit surface roles, tune lighting/contact shadows, keep the kitchen tightly framed, and give render-quality transitions an honest loading state.

### [P1] The configurator exposes its engine instead of guiding a planning decision

Camera modes, editing, AI photo, rendering, environments, layout, finishes, price, sharing, reset, save, and quote appear together. Stage the work as three customer decisions—material, arrangement, review—while keeping the scene persistent. Merge render/environment into a labelled “Ansicht” control, retain text labels for edit/photo, and keep price plus the primary action sticky on mobile.

### [P2] The first viewport undersells the product and its imagery

At desktop width, the hero image begins low and is inset; the first viewport is mostly a warm-white text field. “Premium Küchen” could belong to another brand while “rotpunkt Signature” remains header-scale. Make a real kitchen the dominant full-bleed plane and elevate Signature into the hero-level message, with one headline, one promise, and one primary CTA.

### [P2] German-first voice and form accessibility are inconsistent

Formal “Sie/Ihr” shifts to “deine”; English/internal terms appear beside German copy; checkout fields are placeholder-only and lack durable accessible labels. Establish one formal German vocabulary, rename implementation concepts by customer purpose, add labels/autocomplete/required and error semantics, and preserve the full Signature wordmark on account and quote surfaces.

## Cognitive-load and journey findings

The configurator fails five of eight cognitive-load checks: single focus, chunking, one thing at a time, minimal choices, and progressive disclosure. The desktop scene toolbar alone presents five peers, and desktop simultaneously exposes render, environments, layout, finishes, pricing, sharing, reset, and saving. On a 390×844 mobile viewport, the quote and save actions begin roughly 1,600–1,800px down.

The emotional journey begins quiet and premium, peaks in the material/editorial story and thoughtful account recovery, drops sharply when premium photography becomes a grey Studio scene, and ends at its weakest point in “Fake Checkout”. The peak-end rule therefore fails at the commercial handoff.

## What already works

1. The signed-out account page is the strongest composition: dominant kitchen image, large wordmark, restrained form plane, and a clear one-job surface.
2. The foundations are distinctive: Aspekta, warm gallery neutrals, square geometry, hairlines, scarce signature red, generous measures, and restrained motion.
3. Autosave, local/stale draft recovery, conflict handling, retry, immutable revision restore, and `aria-live` feedback create real operational trust.
4. Browser QA found no horizontal overflow on homepage, configurator, account, or checkout at desktop and mobile widths. The 3D canvas was visible and an actual finish selection updated URL state.

## Design-system direction

Do not replace the authored language with a visual component framework such as Material UI or Chakra. Keep Tailwind plus semantic CSS tokens and `motion/react`; add headless behavior from Radix or Base UI only where accessibility/state machinery is genuinely useful.

Organize the system into three layers:

1. Foundations: semantic color roles, typography roles, spacing/measure, borders, elevation, focus, motion, breakpoints, and z-index.
2. Primitives in `components/design-system`: Button/Link, Field, IconButton, Tabs/SegmentedControl, Dialog/Sheet, Disclosure, Toast/Status, Skeleton, and focus treatment—with idle, hover, focus, pressed, disabled, loading, success, and error states specified.
3. Compositions: AppHeader, SignatureHero, ConfiguratorPanel, SceneToolbar, StickyCommercialBar, ProjectList, QuoteRequestForm, PhotoJobStatus, and shared empty/error/recovery states.

Define three coherent experience modes using the same brand tokens: **Gallery** for persuasion, **Workbench** for configuration, and **Document** for account/quote/review. Each mode may change density and layout, but not vocabulary, form behavior, focus treatment, or brand identity. Sanity content should map only into curated, versioned section props; it should not define layout primitives or runtime interaction behavior.
